When you’re launching or upgrading a restaurant, it’s tempting to cut costs 
wherever possible — and for many owners, that means opting for cheaper 
equipment. On the surface, the savings can seem like a smart move. But in 
reality, buying low-cost restaurant equipment can end up costing you much 
more in the long run.

From unexpected repairs to compromised food quality, the hidden costs of 
cheap equipment can hit your bottom line in ways that aren’t always obvious 
— until it’s too late. Here’s what to consider before choosing the cheapest 
option for your kitchen.
